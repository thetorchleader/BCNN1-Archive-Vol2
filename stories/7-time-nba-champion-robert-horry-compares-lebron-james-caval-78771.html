7-time NBA Champion Robert Horry Compares LeBron James’ Cavaliers to Allen Iverson’s Sixers

June 9, 2015 Sports

Feb 15, 2013; Houston, TX, USA; Shooting stars participant Robert Horry speaks to the media during the a press conference for the all star Saturday night participants at the Hilton Americas. Mandatory Credit: Bob Donnan-USA TODAY Sports

Seven-time NBA champion Robert Horry appeared on “Boomer & Carton” on Monday to talk about the Finals. While praising LeBron James’ incredible playoff performance, he compared his Cleveland Cavaliers team to a couple of other squads led by a single superstar.

“The run that LeBron is doing is impressive,” Horry said. “It reminds me of two different guys: LeBron back in 2007 when he took that team to the Finals — the first Cavs team — and then what Allen Iverson did when he took his Sixers to the Finals. When you got a cast of nobodies, a cast of people you can’t even call on — other than Dikembe Mutombo for the Sixers — to take them to the Finals, put them on your back and do everything offensively and defensively to get them there, it’s special.”

They’re good comparisons in that both that 2007 Cleveland group and the 76ers in 2001 relied on one guy dominating the ball on offense and a total team effort on the other end. With Kyrie Irving and Kevin Love out, it’s obvious that James has needed to have a ridiculous usage rate and make play after play offensively. Like those other teams, though, it’s the other end of the floor where these Cavs are shining most brightly. The role players aren’t putting up huge scoring numbers, but they’re more than held their own on defense throughout the postseason and they’re currently disrupting the Golden State Warriors.

One thing about the teams Horry mentioned, though: they didn’t win it all. Cleveland was swept by the San Antonio Spurs in James’ first trip to the Finals, and Philadelphia lost 4-1 to the Los Angeles Lakers in Iverson’s MVP year. The Cavs have everybody’s attention with this series tied 1-1, but they still need to prove what they’re doing is sustainable.
