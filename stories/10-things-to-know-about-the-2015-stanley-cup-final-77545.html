10 Things to Know About the 2015 Stanley Cup Final

June 3, 2015 Sports

© Getty Images

The Stanley Cup Final is finally upon us. There are many fun facts that come with the Chicago Blackhawks taking on the upstart Tampa Bay Lightning. Here are 10 things you need to know before taking in the final hockey games of the year.

Here are 10 interesting tidbits about the upcoming 2015 Stanley Cup Final to get you ready for the start of the series.

10. TAMPA BAY ONE OF TWO TEAMS PERFECT IN STANLEY CUP FINALS
The Lightning and Colorado Avalanche are the only two teams in the league to play in a Stanley Cup Final and never lose a series. That is the smallest number among the major sports. The Blackhawks will look to cut that number in half.

9. BRAD RICHARDS WON CONN SMYTHE WITH LIGHTNING
This is a storyline that seems to be getting zero coverage. Maybe the Game 7 duo consumed a lot of the analysis, but this is a big story. It has been a long and interesting road since Brad Richards was with the Lightning, but he can relive his glory days if he is able to put together a great series against his former team.

8. TAMPA BAY COULD BREAK LOSSES RECORD
If the Lightning lose this series, they will lose the most games in one playoff year. The inconsistency of their game is to blame for the close calls they have put themselves in. They are playing against the best team they’ve seen so far, so they need to fix the issues they had in series past.

7. 22 CANADIAN PLAYERS, NO CANADIAN TEAMS
The Lightning and Blackhawks are both dominated by players from north of the border. Despite the fanbase being dominant in Canada and most of the talent coming from the Great White North, a Canadian hockey team has not lifted the Cup since 1993. This will be 22 years, the same number as players in these games, since Canada won its trophy.

6. FIRST FULL SEASON WITHOUT MEMBER OF 2004 TEAM GOES TO CUP
With the trade of Martin St. Louis at last year’s trade deadline, the Tampa Bay Lightning lost the last player who won it all as a part of the Lightning. They have a brand new crew, and they can win it all again. They are only four wins away from building a brand new legacy.

5. ALREADY HAD EIGHT OT GAMES
These teams must be tired, because they both have played a ton of minutes. They are on a combined eight games where overtime was needed (five for Hawks, three for Bolts). That shows that both teams do not accept a loss lying down. Expect at least two OT games in this series.

4. DUNCAN KEITH IS AVERAGING MORE THAN HALF A GAME
Duncan Keith is averaging more than 31 minutes of ice time per game. That means on any given night, he is playing more than one and a half periods. He has carried this team to new heights and may be the reason why they are still playing. There are three games in which he cleared well over 40 minutes this postseason.

3. THIS IS THE LAST SEASON WHERE KANE AND TOEWS MAKE DECENT SALARIES
After this season, the Blackhawks will be paying Patrick Kane and Jonathan Toews $ 10.5 million per season. Losing $ 21 million of a $ 71 million cap on two players will be hard to come back from, especially if one is not the goalie. This could prove to be problematic for the Hawks in the years to come.

2. TAMPA COULD BREAK GAMES PLAYED RECORD
The Tampa Bay Lightning come into this series with 20 games played already. The record for most postseason games played is 26. If this series goes seven, then the Lightning will have played 27 games, giving them the longest season in NHL history.

1. LAST FIRST-TIME GOALIE TO WIN CUP WAS A BLACKHAWK
Ben Bishop is trying to win Lord Stanley’s Cup in the first year he’s ever played a playoff game. The last player to do that is ironically Antti Niemi for the Blackhawks back in 2010. Bishop hopes to stick around a little longer than Niemi did (he was traded to San Jose that offseason). He has to win the Cup first to be put into that category.
