10-year-old Boy Who Fought Against Taliban Assassinated As he Walked to School

February 4, 2016 World, zTransfer-UCN

Relatives of an Afghan boy lay his body in the grave during his funeral procession in Uruzgan province of Afghanistan on Wednesday, Feb. 3, 2016. (AP)

Wasil Ahmad once took up arms against the Taliban, fighting alongside his uncle in defending their Afghan village. Local security forces publicly declared that the young boy was a hero, and images of him being honored and showered with flower garlands circulated around social media last year.

But on Wednesday, Afghan officials announced that the 10-year-old boy had been gunned down by insurgents on his way to school, the Associated Press reported.

Wasil gave up the militia and enrolled in the fourth grade just months prior to his death, the New York Times reported.

The boy’s killing comes amid criticism over the use of child fighters in Afghanistan by both government and insurgent forces, a practice forbidden by the country’s law but still prevalent, according to aid groups.

Enforcing the government’s pledges against children in the military has been “slow and tardy,” Child Soldiers International policy and advocacy director Charu Lata Hogg told the Guardian.

The United Nations documented the recruitment and deployment of 68 children by the Afghan national and local police forces, as well as the Taliban,according to a 2015 report — numbers that the agency said were low due to extreme underreporting.

“In a worrisome trend, the Taliban continued to recruit children to carry out suicide attacks and to plant improvised explosive devices, and used them in active combat and as spies,” reads a statement from the Special Representative of the U.N. Secretary-General for Children and Armed Conflict.

Uruzgan province deputy police chief Rahimullah Khan told the AP that Wasil was shot in the capital of Tirin Kot. Wasil’s uncle, Mullah Abdul Samad, once fought as a Taliban commander but switched sides years ago, Khan told the wire service.

Wasil’s father also joined Samad, who was appointed as an Afghan local police commander, according to AP.
