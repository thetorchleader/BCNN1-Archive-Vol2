32 People Injured as Amtrak Train Derails in Kansas

March 14, 2016 National

Passengers gathered after the train derailed near Dodge City, Kan. (Daniel Szczerba via AP)

An Amtrak train heading to Chicago derailed shortly after midnight Monday, sending 32 people to local hospitals, according to the rail agency.

The train was an Amtrak Southwest Chief, part of a route that winds through eight states as it travels between Los Angeles and Chicago. This particular train had left Los Angeles and was about 20 miles west of Dodge City, Kan., when eight of the train’s nine cars derailed just after midnight local time.

There were 145 people on board — 131 of them passengers — when the trail derailed in southwest Kansas and cars careened off the tracks.

Amtrak said 32 riders were taken to hospitals in Dodge City and nearby Garden City; earlier Monday morning, the agency had said 20 riders were injured before increasing that number.

As of mid-Monday morning, 29 of the 32 riders were discharged from the hospital, while three were still receiving treatment. Their conditions were not immediately known, but Amtrak had said earlier Monday that none of the injuries were believed to be life threatening.

The rest of the riders were taken to a recreation center in Cimarron, Kan., and given aid by the Red Cross and other agencies while other transportation options were set up.

A team of investigators from the National Transportation Safety Board was dispatched to Kansas to investigate the derailment. Amtrak says it is working with the NTSB as well as BNSF, the railway that owns and operates the track where the derailment occurred, to investigate the cause.

Due to the derailment, Amtrak said its Southwest Chief train heading from Chicago to Los Angeles on Monday will take a detour.
