17-Year-Old Minnesota Teenager with Autism Suffers Seizure After Police Reportedly Slam Him to the Ground

September 4, 2015 Black

Marcus Abrams shown after the incident with police. Abrams claims that police slammed him to the ground causing him to suffer a seizure yet, he was never charged with a crime.
KARE 11 SCREENSHOT

On Monday, Marcus Abrams, 17, had just left his job at the Minnesota State Fair and was standing on the subway platform with two friends when he claims Metro Transit Police officers approached him.

Abrams, who suffers from autism, told KARE 11 that the he was wearing his headphones and couldn’t hear everything that the officers were asking but he did note that they accused him of being intoxicated.

Then, he claims, things turned violent.

“One grabbed my arm and the other one grabbed my wrist and I told them to get off me — I did nothing wrong,” Abrams told KARE 11. “They just slammed me right on the ground. I tried to get them off me and (one officer had his) whole body on my whole face and I couldn’t breathe.”

During the altercation, Abrams suffered a seizure and was taken to a local hospital.

“I don’t want autism to be a crime, I don’t want people that have it to think that it is a crime. I don’t want people going around saying, ‘oh well if your child is this way they shouldn’t be out here by themselves,'” Abrams mother, Maria Caldwell, told the news station. “He was put in a situation where he was stressed out, a lot of pressure and confused. And I think that is what brought on his seizures.”
