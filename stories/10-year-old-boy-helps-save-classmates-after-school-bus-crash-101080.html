10-Year-Old Boy Helps Save Classmates After School Bus Crashes Into Pond

September 18, 2015 National

Dozens of children went home safe and sound after their school bus careened into a pond Thursday thanks to quick-thinking rescuers and a heroic 10-year-old boy.

All 27 students from Mary E. Bryant Elementary in Florida were scooped to safety as the yellow school bus they were in barreled into the pond, overturned on its side and started filling up with water, police and witnesses said.

It’s still unclear what caused the vehicle to steer off course about 2:20 p.m. near Racetrack Rd. in Odessa, Fla.

The driver was about a mile away from the school when passenger Nicholas Sierra, 10, said the brakes began to fail.

“The bus was out of control and I looked at the brakes, and when he kept on stepping on them, they wouldn’t work,” Sierra told reporters Thursday. “And then it just started accelerating and everyone started screaming and crying.”

The brave student sprang into action, saving his peers one by one.

“I grabbed a kindergartener’s arms and put her around my neck and brought her to land, and then I went back,” he told ABC News.

Meanwhile, first responders and some Good Samaritans also jumped into the murky water to aid the frantic rescue.

“I seen a kid, grabbed him, took him to the bank, seen another kid, grabbed him, took him to the bank,” passerby Tom Batchilder told WFLA News Channel 8. “I probably grabbed a half dozen.”

Batchilder, who works for a pavement company, said he and his partner Vinnie spread out to make sure the students were being saved from both ends of the submerged bus.
