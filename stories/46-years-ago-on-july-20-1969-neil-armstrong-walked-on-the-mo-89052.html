46 Years Ago on July 20, 1969 Neil Armstrong Walked on the Moon

July 26, 2015 Science

On this day in 1969, Neil Armstrong, an American astronaut, became the first person to walk on the moon. Stepping off a lunar landing module, Armstrong told some 1 billion people watching television: “That’s one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind.”

The moon landing originated in a speech that President John F. Kennedy made to a special session of Congress on May 25, 1961, during the Cold War era, when the United States trailed the Soviet Union in space exploration. “I believe,” Kennedy said, “this nation should commit itself to achieving the goal, before this decade is out, of landing a man on the moon and returning him safely to Earth.”

In October 1968, Apollo 7, the first manned spaceflight mission, orbited Earth and successfully tested many of the sophisticated systems needed to conduct a moon journey and landing.

“Buzz” Aldrin subsequently joined Armstrong on the moon’s surface. Together, they took photographs of the terrain, planted a U.S. flag and conducted a few scientific tests. They also spoke with President Richard Nixon via their Houston base. Among the items they left on the moon before returning to their command module was a plaque that read: “Here men from the planet Earth first set foot on the moon — July 1969 A.D. — We came in peace for all mankind.”
