70 World Leaders Meet in London to Raise Money for Syrians Displaced by War

February 4, 2016 World, zTransfer-UCN

British Prime Minister David Cameron and German Chancellor Angela Merkel were among 70 world leaders and diplomats meeting in London on Thursday for a conference that is aiming to raise $9 billion to address the long-term prospects of Syrians displaced by that country’s civil war.

The $9 billion is the amount the United Nations says is needed for economic and educational assistance for the 4.6 million Syrians who have fled to Jordan, Lebanon and other neighboring countries as the conflict in Syria approaches its fifth year. The funds are also needed to help stem the influx of Syrians trying to reach Europe.

Britain has already pledged $1.75 billion in new aid by 2020, and Secretary of State John Kerry was expected to announce a new U.S. commitment later Thursday. Britain, Germany, Norway, Kuwait and the U.N. are the event’s joint hosts.

At the conference, Merkel pledged $1.3 billion toward the effort this year.

Jordan’s King Abdullah this week told the BBC his country, which hosts more than 600,000 Syrian refugees, is at “boiling point.”

He told the conference that one in every five people living in the country is a Syrian refugee, and hosting them consumes a quarter of the national budget.

The gathering comes as Staffan de Mistura, the U.N.’s Special Envoy for Syria, said late Wednesday that there would be a “temporary pause” in the indirect peace talks between Syria’s government and opposition leaders.

The process will resume Feb. 25, he said.

Highlighting the pressure faced by Germany from migrants, the nation’s interior ministry said Thursday that 91,000 asylum seekers arrived in the country in January. The number is lower than the 127,320 migrants who arrived in December, but the decline was mostly attributed to winter weather.

Over 1 million people sought refuge in Germany last year from places such as Syria, Iraq, Afghanistan and other conflict zones. A portion of those are expected to be sent home as Merkel has tightened asylum laws to crack down on migrants who break laws or who don’t meet a test for international protection.

SOURCE: Jane Onyanga-Omara and Kim Hjelmgaard
