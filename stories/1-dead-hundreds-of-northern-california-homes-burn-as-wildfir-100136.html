1 Dead, Hundreds of Northern California Homes Burn as Wildfires Rage On

September 14, 2015 National

A house is engulfed in flames during the Valley fire in Seigler Springs, California on September 13, 2015. The governor of California declared a state of emergency Sunday as raging wildfires spread in the northern part of the drought-ridden US state, forcing thousands to flee the flames. The town of Middletown, population 1,300, was particularly devastated by the Valley Fire, according to local daily Santa Rosa Press-Democrat, which said the fire grew from 50 acres to 10,000 over just five hours Saturday — before quadrupling in size overnight. (PHOTO CREDIT: Josh Edelson/AFP/Getty Images)

A furious wildfire roaring near California’s wine country Monday left behind a scorched trail of burned-out homes and communities as firefighters struggled to slow the blaze’s frenzied pace.

At least one person was dead, several more were unaccounted for and hundreds of houses and other buildings were destroyed as the Valley Fire grew to more than 60,000 acres Monday. The fire, one of more than a dozen racing across the parched state, was only 5% contained, state Department of Forestry and Fire Protection spokesman Daniel Berlant said.

Mark Ghilarducci, director of the California Governor’s Office of Emergency Services, said the Valley Fire and another huge blaze less than 200 miles away alone have destroyed more than 500 homes and forced 23,000 people to flee.

“We are really in a battle with nature, that nature is more powerful than we are,” Gov. Jerry Brown said at a news conference.

Many of the estimated 400 or more homes lost in the Valley Fire are in the Middletown area, where Ross Hardester owns Hardester’s Market. The store, aided by a generator provided by the local power company, remains open to serve firefighters and locals who are riding out the disaster.

“You see it in their eyes,” Hardester told USA TODAY. “Some of them have lost their homes. Some, their homes were saved. But everyone is heartbroken.”

The fire, which forced Brown to declare a state of emergency in Lake and Napa counties, started Saturday afternoon northwest of Sacramento. Four firefighters have been injured battling the blaze.

Details on the death had not been released. Berlant also said “several hundred homes and hundreds of other structures” have been destroyed.

The cause of the Valley Fire, which started near the community of Cobb, is under investigation.

“This has been a very destructive fire,” Berlant said, noting that the towns of Middletown and Hidden Valley Lake have been especially hard hit. Wind-blown embers and ashes have resulted in thousands of acres of drought-parched brush and trees burning in less than 12 hours, he said. “Evacuations are widespread throughout the area,” Berlant said.
