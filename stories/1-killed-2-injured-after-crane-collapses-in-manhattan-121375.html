1 Killed, 2 Injured After Crane Collapses in Manhattan

February 5, 2016 National

A 38-year-old man was killed and three others injured when a construction crane toppled over and smashed into a busy Tribeca street Friday morning, officials said.

The crane crashed into a row of parked cars when it toppled onto Worth St. near Church St. about 8:30 a.m. as heavy snow fell onto the city and winds hit 20 mph.

The person who died was sitting in a car, Mayor de Blasio said. The others were hit by falling debris.

A 45-year-old woman and a 73-year-old man were injured, first responders said.

The woman suffered a leg trauma and the man had a laceration to the head, according to officials.

Both were taken to Bellevue hospital in stable condition.

A third person was taken to New York Presbyterian Hospital in serious condition.

Mayor de Blasio rushed to the scene to assess the damage, setting up a makeshift press office inside the headquarters of the Detectives’ Endowment Association on Worth St.

The accident occurred as workers tried to move the heavy machinery into a secure position because winds topped 20 mph, he said.

“The Department of Buildings had been on the site Thursday because workers were extending the crane — which has a boom length of 565 feet, de Blasio told reporters.
