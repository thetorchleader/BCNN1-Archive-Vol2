4 U.S. Journalists Charged and Released in Bahrain

February 16, 2016 World

Authorities in Bahrain filed criminal charges against four U.S. journalists Tuesday and then released them pending further investigation, the state-run Bahrain News Agency said.

Freelance journalist Anna Therese Day and three unidentified male journalists were arrested Sunday at a demonstration in Sitra marking the fifth anniversary of pro-democracy protests in the tiny nation off the coast of Saudi Arabia. Security forces have been using tear gas and water cannons to break up the protests.

“The individuals were questioned in the presence of lawyers and charged with unlawful obstruction of vehicles and attending unlawful gatherings.” the news agency said. It said the four were released “pending further investigations.”

Jesse Ayala, a friend of Day, told the Associated Press the journalists were doing their job and denied they took part in any “illegal behavior.”

Bahrain, made up of a series of islands in the Persian Gulf, is a strategic military partner of the United States, and the U.S. Navy’s Fifth Fleet is based there. Shiites actually comprise the majority of the population of about 1.3 million people. Bahrain in the past has blamed Shiite-dominated Iran for fomenting unrest in the Sunni kingdom.
